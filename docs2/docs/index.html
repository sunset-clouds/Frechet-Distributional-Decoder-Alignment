what causes the reconstruction-generation discrepancy in latent generative models?

Reconstruction and generation use the same decoder on different latent distributions. Generation-aware reconstruction (GAR) probes how decoder behavior changes along the trajectory from reconstruction to generation, making this transition observable and diagnosable.

01 / Motivation

Two pathways.
Two latent distributions.

Standard reconstruction maps an image to an encoder latent ze ∼ Pe, then asks the decoder to recover the input. Generation starts from noise and produces a generative latent zg ∼ Pg. The same decoder receives inputs from different distributions.

rFID measures decoder behavior under Pe, while gFID depends on behavior under Pg. The evaluation mismatch explains why strong reconstruction can coexist with weak generation.

Comparing reconstruction and generation alone leaves the transition between them unobserved. GAR reveals how decoder behavior changes as its inputs move from encoder latents toward generation-time distributions.

02 / Method

Reconstruction along
the generative trajectory

GAR constructs a controlled trajectory for diagnosing decoder behavior. It perturbs encoder latents, denoises them with the frozen generative model, and probes the decoder on the resulting intermediate latents.

The noise level ηt controls the position along the trajectory: ηt = 0 recovers standard reconstruction, and ηt = 1 recovers generation from pure noise. As ηt increases, correspondence with the source image gradually weakens.

03 / Distributional gap

A measurable path from Pe to Pg

Latent-FD measures the gap between latent distributions in a shared latent space. In the SD-VAE and iMF-B/2 experiment, increasing ηt moves Pgt away from Pe and closer to Pg.

The two distances trace opposite directions across the same GAR trajectory.

toward generation

05 / Structural fidelity

How does source structure change?

GAR outputs reveal the progressive loss of source structure as the trajectory moves from reconstruction toward generation.

This progression exposes the trade-off between generation awareness and source correspondence. Intermediate noise levels provide generation-relevant latents while retaining meaningful paired supervision for decoder adaptation.

06 / Decoder adaptation

From diagnosis to
decoder adaptation

Intermediate GAR latents move toward generation-time distributions while retaining correspondence with their source images. Decoder adaptation uses this paired supervision to fine-tune only the decoder, with the encoder and generative model frozen and no additional inference cost.

07 / Takeaways
  1. Reconstruction and generation use the same decoder under different latent distributions, Pe and Pg, helping explain their performance discrepancy.
  2. GAR constructs and probes intermediate distributions Pgt, making decoder behavior along the reconstruction–generation transition observable and diagnosable.
  3. GAR-FID provides a trajectory-based diagnostic with strong empirical correlation with gFID near the generation endpoint across the evaluated tokenizers and generator scales.
  4. Intermediate GAR latents retain source correspondence, enabling paired supervision for decoder adaptation that improves gFID at every tested iMF scale without additional inference cost.
